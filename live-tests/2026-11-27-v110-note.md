# Lille 1.0.10 — close-the-browser is a direct command; identity by navigation; scripted account switching; continuous session awareness

versionCode 33 / versionName "1.0.10". Built from the
2026-11-27 ~16:26–16:37 on-device reports on 1.0.9
(Ethuram): she STILL could not identify which X
profile was logged in her browser, could not close
the browser when told, had no log-out or
account-switching machinery at all, and — the root
of all three — was not aware of her own live
session: his Connect sign-in had never been read
back, so her accounts registry stood empty about
the session the browser was actually holding.

## What shipped

1. **CLOSE THE BROWSER IS A DIRECT COMMAND**
   (`BrowserControl` grammar + plan; MainActivity
   interception; `BrowserPanel` commanded close).
   "close the browser" and its structural variants
   (close-family verb + browser/panel referent,
   whole-message, leading verb — the kill/resume
   grammar discipline, no phrase lists) are
   intercepted deterministically BEFORE the brain,
   exactly like TaskControl. Effect, in order: the
   work driving the panel halts to PAUSED with a
   "browser closed by you" note on its record (never
   killed — it resumes by word); the panel's
   commanded close is stamped and the panel hides
   immediately (the WebView detaches — he SEES it
   close); one deterministic line lands: "Browser
   closed." With nothing open and no browser work
   active, the answer is still deterministic: "The
   browser isn't open." The commanded close outranks
   the 1.0.3 park-override (his spoken word is
   stronger than the button's dismissal) and holds
   until the task moves — a resume, a handback or a
   new park clears it, and the chip offers the way
   back for live work. Zero model calls; works while
   a reply is in flight. Content ABOUT browsers is
   not a command: "close the browser tab in the
   article" carries extra referent tokens and parses
   as nothing; the grammar is pinned disjoint from
   the kill grammar ("stop the browser" is never a
   kill, "stop the x task" is never a close).

2. **IDENTITY BY NAVIGATION, NOT SCRAPING**
   (`XIdentityRead`). The concluding identity read
   now drives the WebView to X's own-profile route
   (https://x.com/i/profile), lets redirects settle,
   and reads the handle from the RESULTING URL —
   X's own redirect is the witness, and it does not
   depend on any DOM testid existing. A landing on
   the login flow is a signed-out conclusion. The
   1.0.9 page read (shape judgement + scripted menu
   reveal) remains as the corroborating fallback
   when the URL is silent, and as the in-place read
   where navigation is forbidden: a parked page is
   evidence, and a mid-signup flow page must survive
   a resume, so the re-check (parked) and lane-start
   (on a flow URL) reads perform ZERO navigations.
   Navigation yielding neither handle nor login is
   Unreadable with a reason — never a guess. Every
   handback / lane-start / resume / re-check /
   session-tool call site uses the new concluding
   read; the 1.0.9 freshness machinery is untouched.

3. **ACCOUNT SWITCHING + LOG-OUT AS SCRIPTED LANE
   MACHINERY** (`AccountSession`; TaskWorker's
   account-session mode; `TaskRunner`'s session
   pass + acting precondition). The sign-out routine
   is scripted — account menu → Log out → confirm —
   and proven by verify-by-state afterwards (the
   navigation read must conclude signed OUT; the
   taps landing proves nothing). A switch is
   sign-out then sign-in, each a recorded step; the
   registry's active entry flips ONLY on the
   verified reads the read-wrapper applies, so a
   switch that fails halfway leaves the registry
   reading signed OUT, never pointing at an account
   the browser does not hold. HER sign-in is
   autonomous from her vault password (typed as a
   SECRET into the page, never logged); HIS sign-in
   is never attempted — a switch to his account
   signs out and parks for his own login in the
   panel (the existing hand-back flow). THE IDENTITY
   PRECONDITION NOW ACTS: where 1.0.8/1.0.9 stopped
   and named a mismatch, x_read_feed / x_search /
   x_post now execute the switch first (identity
   runs before the action's page opens under
   anyone's name) and stop only when the switch
   itself fails, naming the failed step; a wall
   mid-switch parks as a wall. CREATION-LANE
   UNBLOCK: at SIGNUP_FORM entry — lane start and
   the phase machine's step into it — a browser
   signed in as ANYONE is signed out by the scripted
   routine as a recorded step before the signup
   script posts (his Connect session was the
   creation test's silent blocker); a failed
   sign-out stops the lane with the failure named,
   never a signup under someone else's session.
   CHAT SURFACE: "log out of X" / "log out" and
   "switch to @<handle>" are deterministic commands
   under the same interception discipline (zero
   brain calls) — they resolve to a session pass
   whose verified outcome lands as one deterministic
   line; a switch to his answers with the park step.
   A bare "switch to <word>" intercepts only when
   the word is a registered account — "switch to
   dark" is conversation.

4. **CONTINUOUS SESSION AWARENESS** (`SessionSense`
   + glue). Session awareness is a SENSE, not a
   checkpoint: the browser's sign-in state refreshes
   event-driven — on every page-finish settle on a
   platform domain (BrowserSession.collect feeds the
   sense), on panel open, on app open and on the
   watchdog's passes (reconciliation sweep: the last
   settled page is re-sensed and every stale X stamp
   is owed its live re-read), on handback, and on
   every concluding read. From the sense,
   automatically: the registry syncs (a found
   sign-in registers + activates with the owner
   resolved against her verified handle; a found
   sign-out clears the active entry); a record whose
   stamp CONTRADICTS the sensed reality is corrected
   on the spot with an "identity_sensed" event —
   reality outranks stamps; the StateSheet carries
   the sensed state WITH its sensed-at time; and
   login questions quote the fresh sense first when
   it passes the freshness rule. ONE SOUNDNESS RULE,
   named: a bare profile URL never senses a sign-in
   — resting on x.com/<someone> is VIEWING a page,
   not holding a session; the sense derives only
   from the session's own furniture (the profile
   tab's target, the account switcher's text). The
   navigation read's landed URL is a different
   mechanism (X's own-profile route chose that URL
   for this session) and is not the sense's business.

5. **UNPROMPTED SIGN-IN NOTIFICATION + SESSIONS AS
   THE SAVED CREDENTIAL**. Any sensed session change
   announces itself in the chat the browser work
   belongs to — "Signed in as @<handle>." / "Signed
   out of X." / "Switched to @<handle>." — or is held
   as a pending notification delivered on the next
   app open when no chat owns the work (the "while
   you were away" surface). This generalises the
   1.0.8 handback announcement to ANY sensed change,
   including logins he completes while simply using
   her panel. On a sensed sign-in the registry entry
   is marked session-backed (the persistent browser
   profile IS the credential store; his password is
   never captured — it never leaves the site's page;
   her accounts additionally keep her vault
   password). Account-scoped tasks check the sensed
   session FIRST: a live target session proceeds
   with no login step and no ask; an expired one
   (sensed signed-out, or the entry losing the
   active mark to a switch) marks the entry expired,
   and the next task for that account parks ONCE for
   his login — a second task for the same account
   waits instead of parking again. Multiple accounts
   coexist in the registry; the switching machinery
   moves the single live session between them.
   Facts without a live sensor ride the state sheet
   as last-read values with their age, as before —
   never presented as current.

## Honest limits

- All of this is JVM/CI-proven. The first on-device
  exercise is his install walk: the navigation
  proof's premise (x.com/i/profile redirects a
  signed-in mobile-web session to x.com/<handle>)
  is X's documented own-profile behaviour and the
  route the build's own research verified, but it
  has not yet been observed on his phone.
- If live mobile X exposes none of the assumed
  account-menu / login-flow controls, the scripted
  routines fail NAMING THE STEP (menu, password
  field, verify) instead of guessing — the
  navigation proof is the primary identity source
  precisely because it does not depend on those
  controls; the sign-out/sign-in routines do, and
  their failure mode is a named step, not a wrong
  claim.
- The sense derives a sign-in only where the
  settled page carries the session's own chrome;
  pages that show neither (some X surfaces) refresh
  nothing — the freshness rule + re-check machinery
  remains the backstop, exactly as in 1.0.9.
- Her X account is still not created: this build
  removes the machinery blockers (unverifiable
  session, un-leavable session, no sign-out before
  signup); the creation run itself is the standing
  acceptance test on the installed build.
